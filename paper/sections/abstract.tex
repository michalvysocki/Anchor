Latent world models are trained to predict one step ahead and rolled out recursively, so a latent that does not summarize the history makes long-horizon predictions drift.
Multi-step consistency losses used in practice compare the rollout with the encoded true future, a Monte Carlo target whose variance grows with the horizon, and they apply the same pressure whether or not the latent is Markov.
We propose Anchor, an auxiliary loss without extra parameters that pulls the rollout toward a bootstrapped target started from a later real observation, and we show that the conditional mean of its residual is the rollout bias, so that the disagreement between predictive paths measures the Markov gap of the latent.
We prove that Anchor's gradient vanishes when the latent is Markov and that a small Anchor term strictly improves rollouts under a Markov gap in linear-Gaussian systems, \todo{and show empirically that it improves rollouts under partial observability without harming the well-specified case.}
\end{abstract}
