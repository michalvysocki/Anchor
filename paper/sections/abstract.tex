Latent world models are trained to predict one step ahead and rolled out recursively, so a latent that does not summarize the history makes long-horizon predictions drift.
Multi-step consistency losses used in practice compare the rollout with the encoded true future, a Monte Carlo target whose variance grows with the horizon, and they apply the same pressure whether or not the latent is Markov.
We propose Anchor, an auxiliary loss without extra parameters that pulls the rollout toward a bootstrapped target started from a later real observation, with a strength set by the disagreement between predictive paths, a measure of the Markov gap of the latent.
\todo{We prove that the short-horizon disagreement bounds the bias of long rollouts in linear-Gaussian systems, and show that Anchor improves rollouts under partial observability while switching itself off when the latent is sufficient.}
